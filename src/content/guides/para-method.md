<!-- no-claim -->
PARA sorts everything by how actionable it is, not by subject. A **Project** has a goal and an end date. An **Area** is a standard you keep up indefinitely: health, home, a role at work. A **Resource** is a topic you are interested in. An **Archive** is anything from the other three that is no longer active. Four folders, and the question "where does this go?" answers itself.

## Why it works

<!-- no-claim -->
Subject-based filing puts the mortgage renewal next to articles about mortgages. PARA puts the renewal in Projects, where you will act on it this month, and the articles in Resources, where they wait. Your active list stays short because it holds only projects.

## Set it up in OpenTodo

1. **Four top-level projects**: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives. Create them from the sidebar. [Organise with projects](/docs/tasks-and-projects/#organise-with-projects)
2. **Real projects nest under Projects**, one per outcome with an end date: "Renew the mortgage", "Kitchen renovation". Pick the parent when you create them, or move them later from the project menu. [Organise with projects](/docs/tasks-and-projects/#organise-with-projects)
3. **Areas nest under Areas**, one per standard you maintain: "Health", "Home", "Team". Mark each one, and Areas itself, as an area with **Flow…** in the project's menu: it shows no progress bar, is never called Quiet, and the weekly review stops asking it for a next action. Their tasks repeat; a done area is a contradiction. [Areas](/docs/project-flows/#areas) [Make a task repeat](/docs/recurring-and-reminders/#make-a-task-repeat)
4. **Resources** hold reference tasks and links captured from the browser, with notes. The capture screen lets you pick the project as you save. [Capturing pages](/docs/capture/)
5. **Archive, do not delete.** When a project is done, archive it from its menu: it leaves the sidebar but keeps every task and comes back with one click. OpenTodo even offers to archive a project when you complete its last task. [Archived projects](/docs/trash-and-history/#archived-projects)
6. **See only what is active** with a saved view `##Projects & !completed`, grouped by project; `##` includes sub-projects. [Find tasks with a filter](/docs/labels-and-filters/#find-tasks-with-a-filter)

## Make it stick

- The weekly review is where things move between the four: a stalled project becomes an area or an archive, a resource becomes a project when it gets a date. [The weekly review](/guides/weekly-review/)
- A project you only touch now and then, such as the garden in winter, can be **put in maintenance** instead of archived: its health stops warning and it keeps its tasks. Give it **Review every… month** or **quarter** so it comes up less often. [Maintenance](/docs/review-intervals/#maintenance) [How often a project comes up](/docs/review-intervals/#how-often-a-project-comes-up)
- Quick add understands nested projects: `#Kitchen` finds "Projects › Kitchen renovation" by prefix, and the chip shows the path. [Tokens](/docs/quick-add/#tokens-any-language)
- The [GTD setup template](/templates/gtd-setup/) gives you contexts as labels that work across all four folders. [Labels and filters](/docs/labels-and-filters/)
