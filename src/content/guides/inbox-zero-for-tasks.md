<!-- no-claim -->
Inbox zero is not about having nothing to do. It is about having nothing undecided. An empty inbox means every task you captured has a place, a date or a reason to exist.

## Capture without thinking

Make capturing cheap, so you do it every time:

- [Quick add](/docs/quick-add/) understands dates, projects, labels and priorities as you type.
- Forward emails to your private address with [email to task](/docs/email/).
- Share a page or text from your phone straight into a task ([capture](/docs/capture/)).

## Empty it once a day

Open the Inbox and make one of four decisions for each task:

1. **Do it** if it takes two minutes or less.
2. **Date it** if it belongs to a day; with the [keyboard](/docs/keyboard/) this is a few keys per task.
3. **File it** into a project, with a label for its context ([labels](/docs/labels-and-filters/)).
4. **Delete it** if it no longer matters. It goes to the [trash](/docs/trash-and-history/), so you can change your mind.

<!-- no-claim -->
Five minutes in the morning is usually enough. The goal is an Inbox with nothing in it when you are done.

## Pair it with GTD

Inbox zero is the "clarify" step of Getting Things Done. The [GTD guide](/guides/getting-things-done/) and the [GTD setup template](/templates/gtd-setup/) give you the lists to file into.
